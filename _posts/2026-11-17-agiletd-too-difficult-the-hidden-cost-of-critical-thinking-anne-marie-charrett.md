Critical thinking isn't just a skill problem. It's a social one. And some of us pay more for it than others.

This talk is for anyone who has ever known something important and felt the cost of saying it out loud.

You're in a room. You can see the problem. You summon the courage to say something. The room doesn't move.

You walk out feeling great — maybe people didn't like what you said, but at least you said it. For about a day. Then: was I being too difficult?

And here's the thing that's almost impossible to see when you're in it — this isn't about you at all. Critical thinking is supposed to be a superpower. In practice it gets taxed. And the tax isn't equal — it falls harder on people with less title, less tenure, less resemblance to whoever already gets heard. It's not a personal failing. It's a structural problem. And once you can see it as that, you can stop spending energy in the wrong places.

This talk looks honestly at why fighting the system head-on rarely works — and what happens when you stop looking for the answer where the problem is. We'll draw on Adrienne Marie Brown's Emergent Strategy to explore where leverage actually lives: at the edges, in trusted relationships, in small moves that build over time.

It's not a talk about being braver. It's about being smarter about where your courage goes.

the Critical Thinking Tax

people who are less titled, juniors, minorities, people who are less technical (not said explicitly)

three ways to see this pain, what this tax looks like

- dismissed: bugs dismissed as not important; a senior dev raises it and it immediately gets fixed
- stolen ideas: ideas that are ignored at meetings, 10 min later someone with more tenure raises it and everyone applauds
- character flaw: perceived as aggressive and overbearing when pushing for change and persisting

=> leads to burnout, 48% of workers across eight countries are experiencing burnout, with women disproportionately affected

what can we do about it

do we keep fighting the system

trying to get more credible

stopped shouting and started listening

### recognise the system

- what is rewarded?
- what is measured?
- what is ignored? what is not said in the rooms, what do people not talk about
- where is the real power? is the power in the meeting room, or in a Slack channel, ad-hoc
- what are the levers? Donna Meadow 12 levers which have the greatest impact within a system


### use the system

- use what is rewarded
- use existing structures
- build connections
	Book: Emergent Strategy, Anne-Marie Brand
	=> critical connections, be thoughtful who to connect with in the organisation
- cite standards and process
- customer's voice
- speak as a group: communities of practice
- AI driven analysis: show to the people as is, without judgement and let them decide


### bend the system

- small: small changes, working at a small level (Anne-Marie Brand), hopefully another team will see it
- shift language
- experiments: it is only an experiment, the risk of failing is lower
- change a default: sometimes tools can change human behaviour, ex contract testing to bring feedback earlier
- build on existing momentum
- make the right thing the easy thing
- repeat, repeat, repeat

Testing is a cognitive profession, not a tool-first profession. Using your brain is the basis for everything, for some reason people keep forgetting that.

Let's go back to the basics of what testing is supposed to be doing.
"Oh, how convenient, a machine can now do testing for me, let me send my brain cells on a well-earned holiday".

If that is you, please, don't be like that. As a tester, your brain is your most important asset, so it pains me to see that so many people in the testing community seem eager to offload important cognitive testing tasks to a machine that doesn't care, feel, or can take responsibility.

Using LLM's without question or care is highly irresponsible. This is ironic for us testers because we are supposed to be the critical realists in any project. We should aim to find problems, not blindly let a machine do...whatever.

As an antidote, let's go back to the basics of what testing is supposed to be doing. Let me show you how and why you can use the Context-Driven Testing approach in your work. Using your brain isn't optional for this, it's the basis for everything.

I'll keep repeating this: testing is a cognitive profession, and tools play a smaller role in doing it well than you think.

**Context Driven Testing (CDT)**

> The context-driven testing approach is about doing the best we can with what we get.
>
> Circumstances differ, so the testing approaches will differ
>
> -- James Bach, Brian Marick, Bret Pettichord, Cem Kaner

based on the book: [Taking Testing Seriously](https://app.thestorygraph.com/books/dfb20901-91d6-40f5-9ff9-8df82a0bdeee), James Bach and Michael Bolton

testing cannot ever be complete, catching all the bugs is not possible
=> risk-based testing

## Comparison

Agile Testing vs CDT

| Agile Testing | Context-driven Testing |
|-|-|
| follows principles of agile software development | agnostic of software methodology |
| a practice tied to agile methodology | a testing philosophy, not tied to a certain practice methodology |
| a set of principles | a model for deciding what to test and why |
| can be prescriptive: advocates unit testing, automated testing, testing in the sprint (cfr. scrum) | flexible, depends on context what the tester does |
| team collab | "*people working together are the most important part*" |

agile testing puts a lot of focus on delivery

agile testing: team collab, CDT shares the same idea

Does this matter? No

CDT is about learning, does not assume a context

Agile Testing, assumes the agile context, risks to narrow the testing to the automation side

in practice, agile testing and CDT philosophy can complement each other

what matters more: the degree of freedom you get to shape the testing process

**Remember**: we can be a CDT tester in any type of software development methodology, when we get a large degree of freedom to shape the testing

## CDT principles

1. The value of any practice depends on its context.
2. There are good practices in context, but there are no best practices.
3. People, working together, are the most important part of any project’s
context.
4. Projects unfold over time in ways that are often not predictable.
5. The product is a solution. If the problem isn’t solved, the product doesn’t
work.
6. Good software testing is a challenging intellectual process.
7. Only through judgment and skill, exercised cooperatively throughout the entire project, are we able to do the right things at the right times to effectively test our products.

**Core Skill**: How to create a Test Strategy and why you should care

it is easier to build a story when there is a Test Strategy

- high level: use heuristics to get started
- living document: must be updated on what we learn
- a model: not complete, but an attempt to make our testing priorities explicit

Heuristics

- See it as a way to build a mental model of what matters (open to change!)
- SFDIPOT
- test, take notes, question
- risk-assessment
- Heuristic Test Strategy Model

spend some time using the application, and take notes, feel the questions and write that down

talk to the people who know the product well and let them explain the product to build a mental model

the Test Strategy can be read by the team, they can amend it, do proposals

strategies can change!

Core questions

- why are we here?
- we got the required skills?
- what is hindering good testing?

  -> always try to improve good testing or at least improve what is hindering good testing

- what is already being done

  do not trust existing testing artefacts, they can be wrong

- what is expected from us

Avoid this: a test strategy is not an utopia; do not promise what we cannot deliver. Remember: credibility!

Mitigate: explain the time constraint vs risks we perceive: this is an important story!

Juniors

Start by learning more about risks and risk assessment. Avoid doing "math" with risk.

I see people doing maths with risks, risk mitigation is mostly based on consensus

Mediors/Seniors

should have a strong mental model about our testing approach in any context

**Remember**: a test strategy is based on heuristics it is exploratory, constantly evolves and different by each execution

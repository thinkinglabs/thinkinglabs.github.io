Recently, [Corporate Rebels](https://www.linkedin.com/company/corporate-rebels---the-search-for-the-happy-grail) posted on LinkedIn "[Most workplaces are stuck in the Parent–Child script](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:ugcPost:7521142361360416769/): managers act as parents; employees act as children. Self-managing organisations flip it.
They cultivate Adult–Adult relationships". As much as I would like to confirm this. It has to be a leadership problem, right? For a long time, that was also my firm conviction. However, organisational reality is more nuanced. As confronting as this may seem, for many workplaces, the workplace as a whole — i.e. staff and leadership together — creates and nurtures this Parent-Child relationship.

---

> When we talk about Parent-Child dynamics at work, it’s not a dig at parenting.
>
> The term comes from psychologist Eric Berne.
>
> He showed that in every interaction we slip into one of three “ego states”:
> 👉 Parent: authoritative, nurturing, disciplinarian.
> 👉 Adult: independent, rational, problem-solving.
> 👉 Child: dependent, playful, compliant.
>
> Most workplaces are stuck in the Parent–Child script:
>
> - Managers act as Parents (giving orders, granting approval).
> - Employees act as Children (obeying, complying, seeking permission).
>
> Most of us switch between Parent, Adult, and Child without noticing.
> A sharp tone in a meeting? Parent.
> A sulky silence after feedback? Child.
>
> The problem? That script keeps people dependent, disempowered, and silent.
>
> Self-managing organizations flip it.
> They cultivate Adult–Adult relationships: mutual respect, autonomy, and shared accountability.
>
> This isn’t about judging parents.
> It’s about naming a pattern that still dominates organizational life and showing there’s a healthier way forward.
>
> -- [Corporate Rebels](https://www.linkedin.com/company/corporate-rebels---the-search-for-the-happy-grail), [Oct 28, 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:ugcPost:7521142361360416769/)
